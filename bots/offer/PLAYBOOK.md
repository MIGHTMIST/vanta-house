## THE BOTTLENECK FRAMEWORK (owner, 2 Nov 2026 — every department uses it)

Ask "Why can't we do more?" and walk the steps in order. Stop at the first step that is broken. Fix only that, then start again at the top: it is a cycle.
1. **Metrics** — no numbers = a data constraint. Get the data first.
2. **Market** — are there enough buyers we can reach? (Usually yes: nobody knows we exist.)
3. **Model** — supply or demand constrained? Every business has a hard part ("features vs bugs"); switching just swaps one hard part for another (switch-guard).
4. **Money — "we can't afford to do more"**, three splinters: leads cost too much (creative, volume, funnel, better lead magnet) / we sell too few (messaging, scripts, follow-up, training, offer) / value per customer too low (back end, churn).
5. **Manpower** — not enough people to deliver or sell. Then run the same cycle on getting people.
The hard problem in front of you is the thing you get paid for solving.

## TOP 20% — read first (from the books, read in full 2 Nov 2026)

1. **Make it impossible to compare.** If buyers can compare, they pick the cheapest. Stack value, proof, risk removal and terms until the offer is "a category of one" ($100M Offers pp.43–44).
2. **Value = (Dream × Belief it will happen) ÷ (Time × Effort).** The weakest lever decides what the buyer feels. Push time and effort toward zero; perception counts as much as reality (pp.81–94). Fix the weakest lever first (value-equation-scorer).
3. **Solve every fear the buyer believes will stop them**, sorted under: not worth it, won't work for me, too hard, takes too long. One unsolved fear loses the sale (pp.105–110, 121). Use the buyers' OWN words.
4. **Claim your proof, don't prove your claim.** Collect proof first, then claim only what it shows; if proof is weak, fix reality (Proof Checklist p.4). The site visit is the strongest proof (in person beats virtual).
5. **Risk is the biggest objection — spend the most time on the guarantee** (pp.170, 184). "If you don't get X in Y time, we do Z" (p.172). For big-ticket items avoid unconditional refunds (p.177); prefer conditional ones the client can truly honour (delayed next payment until a milestone p.181, written warranty, release from contract).
6. **Never discount the core. Add bonuses instead** (pp.66, 161; Pricing Playbook p.35 allows a bonus for paying more up front). Each bonus: a benefit name, what it is, the one doubt it kills, proof, a real justified value — never invented "value" totals.
7. **Urgency and scarcity must be true** (pp.146–157): real stock counts, real dated price rises (warn before them, p.157), fixed-date events (p.153), bonuses that really end. REJECT the book's tricks: "someone dropped out" (p.154), deciding "full" yourself (p.150), made-up reasons (p.182). If none is true, say "none yet".
8. **Price talk:** state the full price first, biggest first, then the plan as the easier route (Pricing p.35); every cost agreed up front, never added later (p.46). REJECT the Pricing Playbook's hidden-fee plays (pp.18, 24, 28, 29, 32, 39) — they break "no hidden costs".
9. **A long wait needs visible fast wins** (pp.88–89). Off-plan = a weekly dated progress video.
10. **Free thing = a wrapper, not a price change** (Lost Chapters p.14). Free leads closed at the same rate, ~5x cheaper (pp.25–26). The free thing must solve one narrow problem and reveal the next one, which the offer solves.
11. **The bigger the price, the longer the trust runway** (Lost Chapters p.87): 2 long pieces of real value before the visit/call (78% of Gym Launch's best buyers watched 2+, p.7 — a gym business, so a test for property).
12. **Name it with M-A-G-I-C** (magnetic reason, avatar, goal, interval, container; 3–5 parts). When it fatigues, change the wrapper, not the offer (pp.187–195). No finish date or return number in ad headlines (pp.190–191).

## FROM THE $100M JOURNAL (hand-drawn; offer pages read from page images, 2 Nov 2026)

- **New demand curve (p.50):** bonuses, scarcity, urgency, guarantees and naming push demand up AT THE SAME PRICE. Use it to explain the never-lower-price rule.
- **Offer checklist (p.26):** 1 pre-frame (authority + social proof BEFORE the offer) · 2 price anchor · 3 splinter stack: products, services, and the "next logical need" as bonuses · 4 scarcity (X left) · 5 urgency (by X date) · 6 bold guarantee · 7–8 continuity bonus/downsell (mostly for monthly services). Score every offer against it.
- **6 terms every offer must answer (p.38):** how long · when they pay · what they get · how they know they'll get it · reason to act today · what they have to do.
- **Price-to-value gap (p.33):** buyers quit or stop paying when felt value drops below price. On long payment plans keep value visible (dated progress, milestone gifts).
- **Customer surplus (p.32):** keep what the buyer gets well above the price; raising price while giving less makes buyers feel cheated.
- **Guarantee list (p.48):** results, keep serving, double service, credit, work with you, pay your costs, hotel + airfare, pay for your time, early exit, first outcome, delayed second payment, pay per result. Make one by writing every fear buyers raise and reversing it; the more specific, the better.
- **Headline formula (pp.42–45):** enhancer + duration + dream outcome + power word; M-A-G-I-C. Skip the "% off" enhancer (owner rule).
- Page numbers in the file do NOT match the Journal's own contents page (its lead-magnet pages were not found by number). Lead magnets: use the $100M Leads method in the Marketing skill.

From Lost Chapters for offers: milestone gifts over time — say a gift comes at each stage, keep the exact gift a surprise, promise only what you deliver (pp.109–115); bigger up-front payments mean more buyers finish paying (p.123).

## THE PROCESS (one decision at a time with the owner)

1. **Metrics + buyers' words** from the board; check the Bottleneck tab says the offer is the step to work on.
2. **Dream outcome** — the destination, not the vehicle (p.105). Draft one per buyer type until the avatar is proven.
3. **Problems list** in the order the buyer meets them, before and after buying, in their words (pp.105–108). Say which words come from our own leads and which from press or broker comments.
4. **Solutions → delivery vehicles** (6 levers: group size, DIY/DWY/DFY, support channel and speed, format, hours, 10x/one-tenth test) (pp.117–121).
5. **Trim and stack**: drop low-value items; keep a few high-cost/high-value, the rest low-cost/high-value (checklists, videos, written lists) (pp.122–127). Mark each: who can give it, and "ready today" only when a named person exists; otherwise "Client?" or "Not yet".
6. **Bonuses** (not discounts), **risk removal** (only what the client can sign), **true urgency/scarcity** (or "none yet").
7. **Lead magnet** (Marketing skill, 7 steps) + **pre-visit long pieces**. The client must pass every item a lead magnet invites people to check; ship in steps if some items wait on the client.
8. **Proof today vs proof to collect** (13-point Proof Checklist; GREEN/AMBER/RED). Marketing collects proof.
9. **Client questions** — everything marked "Client?" becomes a fact-only question; the owner decides when to send.
10. **Score**: Value Equation today vs draft; Journal offer checklist and 6 terms; Proof Checklist count with proof that exists today; then an **independent second reviewer** (worker agent or the owner) before anything ships. Fix what it finds, and log the fixes.
