## THE FRAME: $100M Marketing Roadmap (the owner's main marketing playbook — start every job here)

**Two truths.** Every lead problem is one of two things: people don't know you exist, or the few who know you haven't been given enough reason to believe you. Everything we do either makes more people know us or gives them more reason to believe us.

**Step 1 — Is demand really the constraint?** Walk this tree before any marketing work:
1. Can the client handle more customers right now? If no, fix delivery first.
2. Can we afford to get more (spend, post, reach out more today)? If yes, stop planning and do more. It's a volume problem, not a strategy problem.
3. If we can't afford more: do leads cost more than the industry average? If lead cost is normal, the problem is what a customer is worth (Stage 3).
4. Or do too few leads convert? That's a sales problem. Benchmark: in person with a salesperson closes around 30%, webinars 10–15%. Fix that before buying more leads.

**Step 2 — Find the marketing stage.** It's the first row that fits; most people place themselves one stage too high.
| Stage | Constraint | The job | Graduate when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Legitimize | Nobody believes you | Make proof: results, reviews, footage | Proof a stranger can check |
| 2 Publicize | Nobody knows you exist | One channel, Rule of 100 daily, start the list | Leads every week from one channel for 8+ weeks |
| 3 Maximize | Can't afford more customers | Price, churn, margin, better customers, cash forward | LTV:CAC 3:1 in gross profit |
| 4 Systematize | Marketing needs your heroics | Testing machine, kill rule, 70/30, Monday report | A winning ad most weeks without the founder |
| 5 Industrialize | Machine starving for creative | Ad factory, organic engine, AI on written SOPs | 10x creative, same team |
| 6 Magnetize | Ads cost more each year | Brand: epic proof, free value, one message | Buyers arrive already sold |

**Where we are (owner's facts; re-check with data):**
- **Ember itself: Stage 1.** Few documented client results yet. The job is proof. Every client result gets a documented number, a review and raw footage. Don't buy ads to a page with no proof on it.
- **Vanta House campaign: Stage 2.** Real building and real footage exist; leads are uneven. The job is one channel (Meta messages ads + the Page) with daily volume, plus a list of every lead.

**Step 3 — Fix order inside any channel** (the order of leverage):
1. Offer and lead magnet. Most broken channels are broken offers. Nobody wants to "request a quote".
2. Volume. Most broken channels are starved channels.
3. Creative, CTAs, personalization.
4. Targeting. Aim it, then stop touching it.
5. New platforms, last. Add one only once the current channel is crushing it.

**Four rules at every stage:**
- **Volume negates luck.** Results that feel random are too little volume.
- **State the facts and tell the truth.** If the facts aren't strong, change reality (get the result), never the facts.
- **Nobody believes you.** Keep collecting proof forever.
- **Marketing always works; it's a matter of efficiency.** Debug one step at a time: are people clicking, messaging, booking, showing up?

**Stage 1 playbook (Ember):** sell to warm contacts first; trade margin for proof on purpose (if discounting, 80–90% with the condition agreed up front: we get their numbers, before/after and words); record everything while it happens and ask "Where were you before, and where are you now?" at the moment of the win; put proof where strangers find it (Google reviews, real numbers); post 1–3 times a week on one platform as a pulse.

**Stage 2 playbook (Vanta):** marry one channel; Rule of 100 every day, tracked; fix the thing you dangle (the 3 lead magnet types: reveal a problem, free trial, or one step given away; give away something others charge for); every lead goes onto a list that gets a message at least weekly (the book says email; *idea, not from the book:* for Uganda this may be a WhatsApp broadcast list); keep a 1–3 posts a week pulse. Trap: channel-hopping. Stuck signs: you switched channels in the last 60 days, or you have leads you never followed up.

**Stage 3 numbers (when we get there):** lifetime revenue = price ÷ monthly churn; LTV = lifetime revenue × gross margin. Close rate shows underpricing: 80%+ → consider tripling; 60–80% → 2–3x under; 40–50% → 1.25–1.5x under; under 30% → fix selling first. About 20% of customers buy an offer at 5x the price; sell the upgrade at the halfway point.

**Stage 4 machine (for when ad spend grows):**
- Keep testing and scaling in separate campaigns.
- One angle + one format per ad set, 1–4 ads.
- Optimise for the deepest action Meta can see (for Vanta: booked visits, not just messages). One launch: purchase-optimised returned 11x, opt-in-optimised 4x, same ads.
- **Kill rule:** an ad that costs 3× the target cost per qualified lead is dead. Cut sooner if early signs are bad for a couple of days (click-through under about 1.5%).
- Budget 70% on proven winners, 30% on tests. Scale a winner about +25% a day at most.
- About 10 new ads per week per $10k of monthly spend.
- Minimum ante: be willing to spend one customer's acquisition cost to learn if something works.
- Monday report, two questions: is cost normal, is volume normal?
- New funnels lose money before they make it. Budget the bridge or don't start it.

**Stage 5 (later):** assemble ads from parts; kaleidoscope winners into many versions; capture content from what the business already does (site visits, calls, Q&As) instead of making extra; let organic test angles for free; track which ads bring the *best customers*, not just leads; document every process and hand it to agents. Only now add a second channel.

**One-week version:** today run the diagnostic and name the stage; tomorrow pick ONE move and put the daily volume on the calendar; this week do it every day and track actions and results in one place; next Monday kill what missed, double what hit, repeat.

## THE TOP 20% — read this first, it drives 80% of the result

1. **Find the avatar from real buyers, not guesses.** Survey people who already bought. Take the best 20% (spent most, stayed, liked most) and find the 3–5 things they all share. Put those things in every ad, stop selling to people who don't fit, and spend more where the best buyers came from. Then find what they did before buying (one example: 78% watched 2+ long videos first) and make every new lead take that same path on purpose. (Lost Chapters, "Your First Avatar")
2. **Count engaged leads, not likes.** An engaged lead replies, shares a number, or books a visit. Views, likes and followers are not demand. ($100M Leads, "Leads Alone Aren't Enough")
3. **Hooks decide the result.** Put about 80% of ad effort into the first seconds: call out the exact person and promise what they get if they keep watching. Show that person's real setting in the first frame. (Goated Ads, Hooks Playbook, $100M Leads Paid Ads I)
4. **If too few people raise their hand, fix the offer or lead magnet before adding channels.** Give people a free or low-barrier reason to respond. It should solve one narrow problem fast and lead to the next problem, which our core offer solves. (Leads, "Engage Your Leads"; Bonus chapters)
5. **Volume wins.** Rule of 100: 100 primary actions a day (messages, minutes of content, minutes making ads). For ads, make many hooks and build ads from parts: about 50 hooks × 3–5 meats × 1–3 CTAs. (Leads, "More Better New"; Goated Ads)
6. **Find a winner, then multiply it.** About 80% of effort goes on variations of proven ads (remix and remake) and 20% on brand-new ideas. Run a winner until it stops working, not until we're bored of it. (Ads Handbook, "Ad Kaleidoscope")
7. **Track first, then fix one thing at a time at the biggest drop-off.** Order: More → Better → New. One test per platform per week, logged. If 4 tries can't beat the best, move to the next constraint. "New" means a new placement, then a new platform, then a new lead method, and only after More and Better are used up. (Leads, "Core Four On Steroids"; Roadmap Stage 2)
8. **Use proof, not claims.** Real buyers, real site footage, real progress. The closer the proof is to the viewer's situation, the better. Customer proof beats the founder's face. (Ads Handbook "Show Don't Tell"; Marketing Machine; Proof Checklist below)
9. **Judge by buyer quality, not cheapest message.** Optimise value per buyer against cost per buyer, not cost per click. Weak ads and a weak sales step are different problems: if the right people message but don't buy, that's sales. (Ads Handbook "Long Term Result"; Leads Paid Ads II, Lesson #1)
10. **The copy is the targeting, and each ad moves one belief.** Meta's AI (called Andromeda) reads the ad's words, visuals and what is said on camera, then shows it to people likely to respond. Name the exact buyer in the first line and keep targeting broad (the author puts 70–80% of spend on broad). Each ad moves one missing belief: does this work, can I do it, or will the world let me? Each missing belief becomes a family of ads. (How to Write Paid Ads workbook)
11. **Judge ads by cash per lead, after Meta has had time to learn.** Wait at least a few days; Meta wants about 50 results in 14 days to learn. The ad with the most cash (or value) per lead wins, even if its leads cost more. Workbook example: $5 leads made $20 each; $17 leads made $189. (How to Write Paid Ads workbook)
12. **Branding is pairing; price power comes from it, never from discounts.** Branding = pairing our thing with what the ideal buyer already likes; advertising only tells people it exists (Branding Playbook pp.6–7). Trust climbs three steps: what WE say < what OTHER people say (brokers, buyers) < what buyers EXPERIENCE themselves (the site visit) (p.23). Ads win the short term, the product wins the long term: every broken promise turns the brand bad (p.24). One bad pairing spoils the bouquet; fix it by flooding with good pairings, not by hiding (p.29). Brand = pricing power: "if you have to have a prayer session before raising the price by 10%, you've got a terrible business" (Buffett, p.17).
13. **One dated free event beats a vague "come any day".** The Affiliate Black Book is a full kit for one live free event: a fixed date and time, a gift only for people who show up, 6 ad angles (curiosity, explain, social proof, problem/solution, urgency "only happens once", direct benefit), a countdown (21, 14, 7, 4 days, 24 hours, "live now"), friend texts, group and organic posts, and milestone prizes for people who bring others ("we both win when you use my link"). Its writing rules = the owner's: below grade 3, present voice, active, no adverbs, short sentences, positive words, cut extra words.

## PROOF — the review every piece of marketing must pass ($100M Playbook: Proof Checklist, read in full)

**Why it is at the top:** "Your promise is not a differentiator. Your proof is." Anyone can copy a claim; nobody can copy your results. When the author sorted thousands of his ads, 80% of the winners had no education, weren't about the offer, didn't put him centre-stage, and told one story: *this product works.* The losers talked about "us" and "our unique mechanism".

**Two rules before the checklist:**
1. **Claim your proof, don't prove your claim.** Collect the data first, then only make claims the data already supports. If the proof isn't compelling, fix reality (do the work, give free service), never inflate the claim.
2. **Belief is a scale, not yes/no.** Every piece moves the buyer from 100% doubt toward 100% certainty. The best proof is the buyer getting the result before they buy (live demo, free help).

**The 13-point Proof Checklist** — score every ad, post, bot reply, landing page and sales message. The more boxes ticked, the stronger it is. You'll rarely tick all 13.
1. **In person > virtual.** Seeing it with their own eyes wins (for Vanta: the site visit itself is the strongest proof).
2. **Live > recorded.** A real buyer speaking live beats a video of them.
3. **Raw > processed.** A phone-shot, unedited testimonial beats a polished one (same person, same story, raw won).
4. **Show > tell.** Show the result (the building rising, the view, the keys) instead of describing it.
5. **Other people saying it's great > us saying it.** Nobody cares what the seller thinks. Get buyers, reviews, visitors to say it.
6. **Identical to them > opposite of them.** Feature people who look like the buyer (age, gender, background). Want more of a group → show more of that group.
7. **Personal > generic.** Specific before-and-after pain in their own words ("We were two months from shutting our doors"), not "it was good".
8. **Big results > small results > no results.** Lead with the biggest real result; capture it in every format.
9. **Newer > older.** Fresh, dated proof. Old proof makes people think nothing good has happened since.
10. **More > less.** Volume of proof (a wall or page of reviews, a reel of many visitors) makes up for weaker single pieces.
11. **Third-party verified > not verified.** Trusted outside names (approved plans, permits, title, bank, press, Google reviews) — "don't believe me, believe them".
12. **Numbers > no numbers.** Plain numbers, with how and when they were collected; charts beat paragraphs. If the numbers are bad, fix the product.
13. **Metaphors > jargon.** Explain the hard parts (off-plan, title, payment plan) with something they already know.

**How to use it (the oversight step):** before anything ships, a second pass — another bot or the owner — scores it against the 13 points, plus the bullshit meter (GREEN independent / AMBER own word / RED contradicted) and the owner's proof ladder (strangers online < people who haven't done it < people who watched it done < people who did it < people who helped many do it < did it and helped many). Write the score and what's missing. Anything with no proof behind a claim does not ship.

**Proof capture (pair with the Marketing Machine loop):** proof is "like an animal in the wild" — capture it the moment it happens. Record site visits, reactions, key milestones (booking, payment, floor completed, keys). Ask buyers to film their own moments; give them an example and a reason. Keep collecting weekly so proof stays new. Put proof at every step: the ad, the bot's first reply, the site visit, after purchase.

**Vanta proof bank to build (none exists yet — this is the open gap no competitor fills):** dated construction progress video (raw, same angle every week); John on camera (answers "who is behind the money"); approvals and title shown (third party); real visitors' reactions at the site (with permission); buyers who look like the avatar (working Ugandans, people abroad); numbers with dates (floors finished, units sold — only once John shows proof). Never use John's "70% sold" until it is proven.

## Checklists

### 1. Build an ad (hook / meat / CTA)
1. Pick the awareness level: unaware (curiosity), problem-aware (pain), solution-aware (promise), product-aware (proof), most aware (offer).
2. Write about 50 hooks, spread across awareness levels. When in doubt, go a little broader.
3. Write 3–5 meats: demonstration, testimonial, education, story, faceless.
4. Write 1–3 CTAs: show and say what to do, how, when, what they get, and what happens next.
5. Combine hooks × meats × CTAs.
6. Check each one:
   - **Visual:** verbal and visual hook together, movement, a viewer who looks like the buyer, show what you say, captions, platform-native.
   - **Script:** call-out, reason why, dream outcome vs staying the same, speed, ease, proof.
   - **CTA:** one clear next step.
7. Run the 13-point Proof Checklist and get the second-pass review. Fix the weakest boxes.
8. Ship, track, then repeat to find the next winner.

### 2. Write and test hooks
1. Source hooks in this order: our past winners → our free content → other people's ads → other people's content → ad libraries last.
2. Log every hook in the hook sheet: platform, hook, views, link.
3. Split effort 70/20/10: 70% proven hooks, 20% variations modelled on outliers, 10% big new ideas.
4. Make about 10 hooks per ad body.
5. Hook types: label, yes-question, open question, conditional, command, statement, list, story, provocative. Pair the words with a visual or sound hook.
6. After data: take the top 10% of hooks. Next batch = 70% those, 20% permutations of them, 10% new. If there's no data yet, make a batch, then apply this.

### 3. Multiply a winner (kaleidoscope)
- **Remix** (fast, wears out fast): new speed 1.1–1.2x, filter, background, captions or font, headline, static version, new size per placement, new body under the same hook, new effects.
- **Remake** (slower, lasts longer): record again, new props, new examples (unit sizes, prices), new setting, new talent who matches the buyer.
- Do both at the same time. When a new winner appears, put it through the kaleidoscope too.

### 4. The Core Four (only four ways to get leads)
- **Warm outreach** (1-to-1, people who know us)
- **Post content** (1-to-many, warm)
- **Cold outreach** (1-to-1, strangers)
- **Paid ads** (1-to-many, strangers)

Click-to-Messenger ads count as **paid ads**, not cold outreach. Count each pipeline separately. Max out one before adding the next.

### 5. Warm outreach
1. List every contact: phone, email, socials, Page engagers, past enquirers.
2. Start with the platform where we have the most contacts.
3. Open with something personal. No pitch.
4. Reach 100 a day, up to 3 tries each (1 per day).
5. When they reply, use A-C-A: Acknowledge, Compliment, Ask the next question.
6. After 3–4 messages, make a light offer: "Do you know anyone who…?"
7. Keep the list warm. Test now and then with "Are you still looking to [4-word desire]?"

### 6. Cold outreach
1. Build a targeted list first (groups and communities give the freshest leads).
2. Research each lead. Open with 1–3 personal facts.
3. Lead with big, fast, free value.
4. Keep scripts short. Send the first 100 before tweaking.
5. Follow up more times and in more ways.
6. Re-run the list after 3–6 months.
7. Track every number daily.

Templates:
- 9-word message: "Are you still looking to [4-word desire]?" (drop "still" for first contact)
- Follow-ups: Day 1 call + message, Day 2 call, Day 3 message, Day 4 text, Days 4–7 call, Day 8 final message.

Only 9 AM–5 PM. Check local rules on cold messaging.

### 7. Lead magnet (7 steps)
1. Pick one narrow problem and who has it. The core offer must solve the next problem it reveals.
2. Pick a type: reveal a problem, a sample or trial, or one step of a process.
3. Pick a delivery: tool, information, service, or physical item.
4. Test names: headline first, then image.
5. Make it easy to use.
6. Make it truly good: give away the secrets, sell the doing.
7. Clear CTA plus a real reason to act now (true scarcity or a deadline).

Free brings the most leads. If too many time-wasters come, add friction (qualifying questions, prices shown early) rather than killing the offer.

### 8. Content
- Every post = **Hook** (get noticed) → **Retain** (list, steps, story) → **Reward** (deliver the promise).
- Headline: at least two of recency, relevance, proximity, conflict, unusual, celebrity, ongoing.
- Match the best format on that platform.
- Give far more than you ask. Ask after a valuable moment.
- Keep a list of top posts. Post manually where possible.

### 9. Marketing Machine (weekly proof loop)
Every week, collect real proof into labelled folders, **then use it in ads**. A folder alone gets no customers.

Sources: chat screenshots, Page comments and reviews, tagged posts, visitor reactions on site (with permission), construction progress.

Testimonial script (6 points):
1. Inner struggle
2. Outer struggle
3. Doubt
4. Why they did it anyway
5. Outer win
6. Inner win

Hook first. Name at the start. Get permission, and honour a no.

Do one source at a time ("One > None").

### 10. Avatar survey (do this first for every client)
1. Ask the client for what they know about past buyers: where they live, age, what they own or do, why they bought now, how they first heard, what they watched or read first, and whether someone referred them.
2. Sort for the best 20%: spent most, stayed, easiest to deal with.
3. List the 3–5 things they share.
4. Put those things in every ad and drop people who don't fit.
5. Copy their path: whatever they saw before buying, send it to every new lead before the visit or call.

### 11. Friction dial (lead quality vs volume)
Order: get flow first, then make money from it, then add friction. Five levers, from lightest to heaviest:
1. Qualifiers in the ad (price, area, who it's for)
2. More or heavier questions (budget or phone number is heavier than a name)
3. More steps
4. Forced watching (a video before the next step)
5. A longer ad before the CTA

Change one lever at a time and watch the data. Free leads are not automatically bad: in the book's tests, free leads closed at the same rate and cost about 5× less. Test it, don't assume it.

### 12. When nobody responds to a free offer, find the cause in this order
1. They don't want it, or it's described badly.
2. They don't believe it. Give a true reason the offer exists.
3. Wrong pond. It's a targeting problem.

### 13. Content units (for longer videos and posts)
A long video is a chain of units: hook → retain (list, steps or story) → reward, repeated. Every unit gets its own hook and reward. Place the offer between units, not only at the end. (Journal)

### 14. Hook angles: What-Who-When
- **What:** more of the dream (fast, easy, likely) or less of the nightmare (slow, hard, risky)
- **Who:** themselves, family, friends, colleagues, rivals
- **When:** past, present, future

(Journal, read from a drawing, so treat it as a prompt for angles.)

### 15. Lead getters (other people's customers)
Find partners who already serve our buyers and give them a free thing to hand out, or split the money when they bring a buyer. Go where the people with the problem already gather. (Journal; Lost Chapters "Employees")

### 16. Research before writing (do it once per client, keep it updated)
1. **Story Bible:** one doc with the offer, price, the buyers, the proof bank, objections, funnel links, the 6 Ps, approved and banned claims, and how the founder talks. Every ad is checked against it, and every client edit updates it.
2. **Why They Buy sheet:** best buyers' exact words from chats, calls, comments and reviews. Never reword them. Tag each line: why they bought, want, pain, or objection.
3. **Objection bank:** every reason people haven't bought, next to the part of the offer that answers it. Every row is an ad.
4. **Market check:** competitors in the Facebook Ads Library. Ads running for months mean there's money in the market. Read 3-star reviews to find what's missing.
5. **Awareness level** (unaware → most aware) decides how the ad starts. **Sophistication stage** (how often buyers have heard the claim) decides whether we need a mechanism: "Everyone thinks X. It's wrong because Y. So the answer is Z."
6. **Big idea:** one idea above all the angles. It should be new, easy, safe and big.

### 17. Copy rules (6 Ps and 5 Cs)
Answer the 6 Ps before writing:
- **People:** who exactly? Describe their pain better than they can.
- **Positioning:** why us? Be the first to tell true facts others don't say.
- **Promise:** run "so what?" until you reach their life. "By the end, you'll have ___, so you can ___."
- **Proof:** if you can't point at it, you can't claim it.
- **Priority:** why now? True scarcity, a deadline, or the cost of waiting 12 months.
- **Process:** "Everyone thinks X, the problem is Y, the fix is Z."

Write with the 5 Cs:
- **Clear:** grade 6 or lower.
- **Concise:** if every word cost $10, what survives?
- **Concrete:** numbers, names, things you can picture.
- **Conversational:** how the buyer would say it.
- **Cadence:** vary sentence length and read it aloud.

Hook score: Benefit × Relevance × Credibility ÷ Effort. Score each hook 1–5 per part and pick the highest. Write the *moment* ("if you've ever…"), not the thesis. Open a loop instead of closing it.

Body: about 180–210 words, one belief. Formats: story (use "but" and "therefore", not "and then"), What-Why-How, analogy, steps, testimonial.

CTA: one CTA, one avatar, one offer. Who + what + when + how + what they get.

Before shipping, run these passes:
1. **Clear or clever:** cut anything that only looks clever.
2. **The 5 Cs.**
3. **Voice pass:** does it sound like the founder?
4. **Kill pass:** no "it's not X, it's Y", no lists of 3 just for rhythm, no exaggeration.
5. **Compliance:** Meta reads words at face value, so no guarantees you can't back up.
6. **Proof pass:** the 13-point Proof Checklist and the second-pass review.

Truth rules:
- State the facts. Never use fake urgency, fake timers or hype. One broken promise spreads as bad word of mouth.
- A **damaging admission** builds trust: admit what you're not, then say the one thing you are great at.
- Good copy is a window. The reader should see the offer, not admire the writing.

### 18. Ad batch and testing plan
- Per ad: a title, 5 hooks, 1 body, 3 CTAs. Write the hooks first.
- A batch of 10 is spread across different beliefs and angles. No single hook format takes more than about a third of the batch.
- **Test angles with static images first.** Only film a video for an angle that has already won.
- Film one body plus a stack of hooks (kaleidoscope). Change one thing at a time so we know why something won.
- Creative volume, as a rule of thumb: 15–30 pieces for every $10k of spend.
- Tag every ad so its leads can be traced back to it.
- After each batch, write down what won, what the client changed, and the rule that comes out of it. Add that rule to the Story Bible.
- Explore with organic posts, then exploit with paid. A winning post plus a CTA is often the easiest ad.
- **Make ads look and sound like good organic content.** Meta wants people to stay on the platform, so an ad that looks like an ad gets shown less.
- **Iterate on the winning angle, not on shirts or backgrounds.** Say the same winning angle in new ways. A new background barely counts as a new ad.
- **Stop rebuilding campaigns.** Give each one time to learn (about 50 results in 14 days).
- Free, useful organic content builds goodwill, and ads to the most-aware people then skim it off the top.
- **Use AI for research and batch review.** After a batch, ask what we are doing too much of: the same CTA everywhere, one awareness level, one angle? (The author writes copy himself; for us, Claude writes and the owner judges.)

### 19. Content as proof (public challenge)
1. Write down who we want to reach.
2. List the traits they must see in us.
3. Show each trait in content, on a set schedule.
4. Content takes longest to work, but it keeps working.

### 21. Branding check (Branding Playbook, read in full 3 Nov 2026)
1. Who: the ideal buyer, by the 4 tests (growing, in pain, can afford it, easy to find) (pp.19–20).
2. What they like: from their own words and data, not guesses (p.21).
3. Pair with it through content they like, products they like (free and paid), and people they like (pp.22–23, one-page checklist p.33).
4. Climb the authority steps: what we say → what others say (brokers, real visitors, buyers) → what they experience (the visit). Put most effort on the last two.
5. Cut bad pairings: anything the ideal buyer dislikes (for Vanta: AI-looking footage on proof videos, "China Speed" until tested, discount words, yield promises, "low investment"). Keep the bouquet narrow: content that pulls the wrong viewers (e.g. TikTok's young crowd) is the wrong views.
6. Make the product match the promise, or don't make the promise (amenities in writing, dated progress).
7. Measure: reach (how many know), influence (does it change behaviour) and direction (towards or away) — in money: do the right people buy?

### 22. Event campaign (Affiliate Black Book, read 3 Nov 2026)
1. One fixed date, time and place; free; a real gift that only people who come get (never invented).
2. Six ad or post angles: curiosity, explain, social proof (only if true), problem/solution, "only happens once" (only if true), direct benefit.
3. Countdown to everyone registered: 21, 14, 7, 4 days, 24 hours, and "starting now" (WhatsApp, not email, in Kampala).
4. Friend scripts and group posts people can forward; milestone rewards for people who bring others (real rewards, approved by the client, in writing).
5. Grade-3 writing rules (see Top 20% #13).
6. Free channels first (existing leads, groups joined, the page) when the owner has paused ad money.

## Metrics to track (put them on the chart)

- **Engaged leads** per day, per source, per ad.
- **Funnel by step:** impressions → messages → qualified (budget fits) → visit booked → visit attended → reservation → sale. The constraint is the step with the biggest drop-off.
- **Cost per message, cost per qualified lead, cost per booked visit, cost per attended visit**, all from real ad spend.
- **Hook log:** views per hook. Top 10% = what to copy.
- **Test log:** one test per platform per week, with the result written down every Monday.
- **Cash per lead (main ad judge):** value or cash from an ad's leads ÷ its number of leads. For Vanta, use booked or attended visits per ad until sales data exists.
- **Qualified leads per dollar.** Compare campaigns by raw qualified leads for the money, not by % qualified. Example from the book: 250 qualified for $1,000 beats 160 qualified for $1,000.
- **Full cost per channel:** ad spend + bot and tool costs + anyone's paid time. "Free" content is not zero.
- **5 lead stages:** uncontacted → contacted → engaged → qualified → sold. Track the conversion number at each stage.
- **Ads vs sales check:** if cost per buyer is high, ask: "Do our engaged leads have the need and the money?" No = an ads problem. Yes but not buying = a sales problem.
- **Commission per unit ÷ cost to get a buyer.** This stands in for LTGP:CAC; the book targets 3:1 or better.
- **Ad test budget** (Leads, Paid Ads II): up to 2× what one customer brings in. Kill at 1× if zero leads.
  - *Estimate needed:* Vanta's commission is paid per unit sold, not within 30 days. Until the commission figure is in writing, use cost per booked visit as the early signal.
- **Proof score:** the 13-point Proof Checklist score for each ad; compare winners vs losers.

## Warnings from the books

- Don't add platforms before maxing one. Facebook + Instagram + TikTok all at once is "width first": more work, often weak content everywhere. This is a trade-off to decide with the owner, not a ban.
- Don't switch too early. The book's own cold outreach took almost a year to scale, and winners in paid ads can take many losers first.
- Don't spend before tracking is set up.
- Don't make too few ads. 5 a month hit a ceiling, and the ceiling was ad quality, not the market.
- Don't judge an ad by cheapest lead alone, and don't blame ads for a sales problem.
- Check ad status weekly. Rejected ads can sit unnoticed.
- Don't make ads about "us" (our mechanism, our features). The Proof Checklist author's losing ads did exactly that; the winners told one story: it works.
